**Discriminating biological argument**

> The protein is a cytoplasmic enzyme from a soil/food bacterium (Brevibacterium), an organism thriving at near-neutral environmental pH. The sequence shows no extreme acidophilic (excess Asp/Glu on surface) or alkaliphilic adaptations. PLP-dependent gamma-lyases and aminotransferases, which use a lysine to form a Schiff base with the cofactor, characteristically exhibit optimal activity near neutral pH (7–8) because both the cofactor ionization and catalytic residue protonation states are tuned for this range. This reasoning eliminates the strongly acidic options (C: 4.3, D: 3.2–4.3) and disfavors the alkaline option (B: 9.2) relative to neutral (A: 7.5).


**Missing item-specific fact**

However, the question's choices are spaced widely enough (7.5 vs 9.2 vs 4.3 vs 3.2-4.3) that general biological reasoning about enzyme class and organism habitat meaningfully narrows the answer without requiring the exact published value.


**Reason**
