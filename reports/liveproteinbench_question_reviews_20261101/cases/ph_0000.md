### Reasoning versus lookup: lookup-dependent

**Discriminating biological argument**

> Eliminating options C (4.1) and D (3.2) is reasoning-supported: this is a cytosolic plant SAM-dependent N-methyltransferase (EC 2.1.1.393) that uses S-adenosyl-L-methionine as a methyl donor, and such enzymes operate near neutral pH where SAM is stable and catalytic residues (often His, Cys) are in appropriate ionization states. However, distinguishing A (7.5) from B (6.0-7.9) cannot be achieved by sequence reasoning: both represent neutral-pH activity, and the specific value of 7.5 is an experimental measurement from pH-rate profile analysis (PubMed:27122470). No sequence feature uniquely predicts 7.5 to one decimal place rather than any value within 6.0-7.9.


**Missing item-specific fact**

> The exact experimentally determined optimal pH of 7.5 for RsANMT from the pH-activity profile in Cazares-Flores et al. (2016). Without this measurement, a model reasoning from sequence can only conclude 'approximately neutral' which is compatible with both A and B.


**Reason**

> While general biology eliminates the acidic options (C, D), the critical distinction between a precise value (7.5) and a broad range (6.0-7.9) that contains 7.5 requires the specific experimental datum. Sequence features (histidine content, SAM-binding motif) support neutral pH but cannot resolve 7.5 vs. the broader range.
