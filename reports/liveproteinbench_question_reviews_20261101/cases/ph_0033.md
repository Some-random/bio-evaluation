### Reasoning versus lookup: lookup-dependent

**Discriminating biological argument**

> A weak contextual argument exists: the protein contains EF-hand calcium-binding motifs and is secreted (signal peptide present), suggesting it functions in a physiological extracellular environment near neutral pH. Additionally, one might note the absence of a canonical trypsin-like catalytic triad arrangement that would suggest a specific active-site pKa environment. However, these observations cannot reliably distinguish pH 7.0 from pH 9.0 or the alkaline ranges in choices C/D without knowing the protein's identity.


**Missing item-specific fact**

> The experimentally measured optimum pH of Longistatin (7.0) from PubMed:22206819. Without identifying the protein as Longistatin and recalling its characterized biophysical properties, the sequence alone does not provide a clear mechanistic basis to select 7.0 over 9.0 or the alkaline ranges. The 'unconventional serine protease' designation means standard active-site pKa reasoning for classical serine proteases does not straightforwardly apply.


**Reason**

> Predicting an exact pH optimum (7.0 vs 9.0) from a 156-aa sequence lacking an obvious canonical catalytic triad requires either (a) identifying the protein as Longistatin and recalling its published experimental pH optimum, or (b) organism-level biological context (tick feeding on mammalian blood at pH ~7.4) that is not derivable from the sequence alone. The sequence composition (balanced acidic/basic residues, EF-hand motifs, signal peptide) is insufficient to discriminate between neutral and alkaline pH optima with confidence.
