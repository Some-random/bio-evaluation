### Reasoning versus lookup: reasoning-supported

**Discriminating biological argument**

> The sequence contains a Rossmann-fold NAD-binding motif (TGAGFIGSNLLE resembling GxGxxG), identifying this as an NAD-dependent UDP-hexose 4-epimerase. Such enzymes catalyze a reversible NAD-dependent oxidation/reduction at C4, a mechanism that does not require extreme protonation states and operates near neutral pH. The organism (Pseudomonas aeruginosa) is a neutral-pH bacterium, and the protein is cytoplasmic, further supporting a near-neutral optimum. This reasoning eliminates A (9.7, strongly basic) and C (3.2, strongly acidic). The question explicitly asks to 'predict the pH range,' and general enzyme characterization conventionally reports optima as ranges rather than single values, favoring D (7.0-8.0) over B (7.4). The NAD-dependent epimerase mechanism provides no structural rationale for a sharply defined single-pH optimum.


**Missing item-specific fact**

> The precise experimental boundaries of the optimum (7.0 vs 7.4 vs 8.0) are determined by assay measurement (PubMed:10747995) and cannot be derived from sequence alone. However, the broad neutral-pH prediction and the range-vs-single-value format are inferable from general biological knowledge.


**Reason**

> Elimination of extreme-pH distractors (A, C) follows from the NAD-dependent epimerase mechanism and neutral-pH bacterial cytoplasm context. The preference for a range (D) over a single value (B) is supported by the question's phrasing ('predict the pH range') and the convention that enzyme pH optima are reported as ranges. While the exact boundaries (7-8) are experimentally determined, the qualitative prediction of 'neutral, approximately 7-8' is accessible through biological reasoning about the enzyme class and organism.
