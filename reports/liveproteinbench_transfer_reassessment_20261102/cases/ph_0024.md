The finer distinction between 8.5 and 8.9 rests on mechanistic knowledge of PGI catalysis learned from homologs: PGI uses a conserved active-site His as a general acid/base catalyst (pKa typically 7–8.5 in PGI enzymes across species) and a Lys for substrate binding (pKa ~10). The pH optimum is governed primarily by the His pKa. Even in hyperthermophilic PGIs, the increased salt-bridge network shifts the His pKa modestly upward but rarely beyond ~8.5–8.7. Across characterized PGI enzymes (E. coli, yeast, human, thermophilic bacteria), reported optima cluster in the 7.0–8.5 range. A value of 8.9 would require an unusually large pKa shift inconsistent with the conserved PGI catalytic mechanism. Thus, family-level mechanistic knowledge and the statistical clustering of PGI pH optima provide a credible, though probabilistic, basis to prefer 8.5 over 8.9.
