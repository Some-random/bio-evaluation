At pH 3.2–4.3 (options C and D), the PLP Schiff base (internal aldimine) would be hydrolyzed, active-site lysine and histidine would be fully protonated, and the enzyme would denature—eliminating both acidic choices. Between 7.5 and 9.2: at pH 9.2 the pyridine nitrogen (>50% deprotonated at its pKa ~8.3) loses the positive charge needed for electrophilic catalysis, and the vast majority of characterized bacterial PLP-dependent enzymes (aspartate aminotransferase, cystathionine beta-synthase, serine dehydratase, etc.) peak at pH 7.0–8.5. A pH of 9.2 is atypically high for this enzyme class. Combined with the organism's neutral-pH habitat (Brevibacterium, soil bacterium), the family-level statistical pattern and PLP pKa chemistry meaningfully favor 7.5 over 9.2.
