A well-established biochemical principle is that when histidines are catalytic, the optimal pH tends to cluster near the histidine side-chain pKa (~6.0-6.5 in free solution, often 5.5-7.0 in protein environments). This family-level inference (histidine-dependent catalysis → pH optimum near His pKa) meaningfully disfavors the alkaline options A (8.5) and D (8.0), where histidines would be fully deprotonated and unable to act as general acid catalysts. It also provides a probabilistic lean toward C (6.3-6.5) over B (7.3), since the histidine pKa center is closer to 6.3-6.5. Additionally, the small size of the protein (107 aa) limits the electrostatic buffering capacity, making the enzyme's pH optimum more directly tied to individual catalytic residue pKa values rather than broad electrostatic stabilization, further supporting a pH near the histidine pKa.
